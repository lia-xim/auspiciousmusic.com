Authors

Every name on this site belongs to a real person who wrote or reviewed the piece it appears on. That rule is why this page is currently short.

The byline rule

Invented authors are among the cheapest ways to make a publication look established, and among the fastest ways to destroy its credibility. This site does not use them. No article carries a name unless a real person wrote or reviewed it, and author pages link to work that can be checked.

Until individual author records exist, pieces are credited to the editorial desk. That is an accurate description of who is answerable for them, not a placeholder standing in for a person.

What an author page will contain

  • Full name, and the areas the person actually works in.
  • Verifiable background — released work, credits, or a professional profile that can be checked independently.
  • Every article written or reviewed by that person, with the review role stated where it differs from authorship.
  • Any commercial relationship that could bear on what they write about.

Reviewers

Technical pieces may carry a reviewer in addition to an author — someone who checked the procedure rather than wrote it up. Where that happens the reviewer is named on the article with the date of the review, because a review from three years ago says something different about a piece of software than one from last month.

The standards these credits follow are set out in the editorial policy.
