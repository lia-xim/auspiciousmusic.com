How to use it

Set the tempo, then take the value for the division you want. A 1/8 delay on a rhythmic part usually wants the straight column; a delay that should sit slightly behind the groove without landing on the next note is what the dotted column is for — dotted 1/8 is the reason a great many records sound the way they do.

The hertz column is for anything that takes a rate rather than a time: LFOs, tremolo, auto-pan, and any modulation you want locked to the grid in a plug-in that has no tempo sync.

Reverb pre-delay and decay

The two reverb read-outs are starting points, not rules. Pre-delay at a 1/64 note keeps the early reflections clear of the transient without drifting audibly late. The decay figure is the length of one bar in 4/4 — set the tail near it and the reverb clears before the next downbeat rather than smearing across it.

Both need adjusting by ear against the actual material. A dense arrangement usually wants a shorter tail than the arithmetic suggests.

The arithmetic

One quarter note lasts 60000 ÷ BPM milliseconds. Every other division is that value multiplied or divided by powers of two. A dotted note is one and a half times its straight length; a triplet is two thirds. Frequency in hertz is 1000 ÷ ms.
